Appendix A

The math in one place

Every equation the controller runs, in the order it runs them, with Talos's numbers. Nothing here is new: each block links back to the chapter that explains it.

The model

Explained in Chapter 1 · fossen_model.cpp

State and kinematics

x = (p,\ q,\ v,\ \omega) \in \R^{13}, \qquad \dot p = R(q)\, v, \qquad \dot q = \tfrac12\, q \otimes \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}

Dynamics

\begin{aligned} \M\,\dot{\bnu} &= \btau_{\text{thrust}} + \btau_d - \mathbf C_{RB}(\bnu)\bnu - \mathbf C_A(\bnu)\bnu \\ &\quad - \phi\,\mathbf D(\bnu)\bnu - \mathbf g(\bEta) \end{aligned}
\M = \M_{RB} + \M_A, \qquad \M_{RB} = \begin{bmatrix} m I_3 & 0 \\ 0 & I_g \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \M_A = \operatorname{diag}(X_{\dot u}, \dots, N_{\dot r})

Coriolis in momentum form, for \M_{RB} and \M_A alike, with (h_1, h_2) = \M\bnu:

\mathbf C(\M, \bnu)\,\bnu = \begin{bmatrix} \omega \times h_1 \\ v \times h_1 + \omega \times h_2 \end{bmatrix}
\mathbf D(\bnu)\bnu = \mathbf D_l\, \bnu + \mathbf D_q\, |\bnu| \circ \bnu \qquad (\text{both diagonal})
\mathbf g(\bEta) = -\begin{bmatrix} R(q)^\top \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\ \phi B - W\end{bmatrix} \\[4pt] r_b \times R(q)^\top \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\ \phi B\end{bmatrix} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{aligned} W &= mg \\ B &= \rho g \nabla \end{aligned}
\btau_d = \begin{bmatrix} R(q)^\top \hat d_F \\ \hat d_\tau \end{bmatrix}

\phi \in [0, 1] is the wetted fraction of a buoyancy ellipsoid; it is 1 everywhere except at the surface, where it also moves r_b toward the wet part.

One integration step

Explained in Chapter 1

\operatorname{step}_h:\quad f \xrightarrow{\ h/2\ } f', \qquad x \xrightarrow{\ \text{RK4},\ f'\ \text{held}\ } x^+, \qquad f' \xrightarrow{\ h/2\ } f^+

h = 2 ms for predictions, h = 25 ms for Jacobians. One stage F(x_k, u_k) is \Delta t / h steps with u_k held.

Thrusters

Explained in Chapter 2 · thruster_dynamics.cpp, hardware_config.cpp

Thruster matrix

\btau_{\text{thrust}} = \T f, \qquad \T_{:,i} = \begin{bmatrix} d_i \\ r_i \times d_i \end{bmatrix}

d_i is the unit thrust axis and r_i the position relative to the COM, both from CAD. Near the surface each column is scaled by its disk's wetted fraction.

Delay, lag, limits

f_{\text{target}}(t) = u(t - 30\ \text{ms})
f \leftarrow f + \operatorname{clamp}\!\big((f_{\text{target}} - f)(1 - e^{-h/\tau}),\ \pm 300\,h\big), \qquad \tau = \begin{cases} 80\ \text{ms} & \text{spinning up} \\ 60\ \text{ms} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
|u_i| \le 24\ \text{N}, \qquad \textstyle\sum_i |u_i| > 73\ \text{N} \ \Rightarrow\ u \leftarrow u \cdot \dfrac{73}{\sum_i |u_i|}

Allocation (feedforward only)

Explained in Chapter 2

f = \T^{+}\btau = \T^\top(\T\T^\top)^{-1}\btau

Force to RPM

Explained in Chapter 2 · Blue Robotics T200 data

|F| = K_T\,\rho D^4 \Big(\frac{n}{60}\Big)^2, \qquad K_T = a + b\,n

Inverted for n with a few Newton iterations.

Reference generation

Explained in Chapter 4 · motion_profile.cpp, path_plan.cpp

S-curve

Piecewise-constant jerk on a triple integrator, re-chosen every 2 ms: accelerate toward v_{\max}, but brake once the stopping distance from one substep ahead exceeds the distance left. With no hold phase:

d_{\text{stop}}(v) = v\sqrt{v/j}, \qquad a_{\text{peak}} = \min\!\big(\sqrt{jv},\ a_{\max}\big)

Inside the capture region (2 mm, 2 cm/s, 0.1 m/s²), a triple pole at -p:

j = p^3 e - 3p^2 v - 3p\, a, \qquad p = 10\ \text{s}^{-1}

Limits for a move at angle \theta from horizontal:

\frac{1}{L(\theta)} = \sqrt{\Big(\frac{\cos\theta}{L_h}\Big)^2 + \Big(\frac{\sin\theta}{L_v}\Big)^2}

Path speed envelope

Explained in Chapter 4

v(s) \le \min\Big\{ \frac{v_{\max}}{|dp/ds|},\ \frac{\omega_{\max}}{|d\psi/ds|},\ \sqrt{\frac{a_{\text{lat}}}{\kappa(s)}},\ \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{\max}}{2\,|d^2\psi/ds^2|}} \Big\}

followed by a backward pass so the profile can always brake in time.

Reference governor

Explained in Chapter 4

v_{\max} \leftarrow \sigma\, v_{\max}, \qquad \sigma = \operatorname{clamp}\!\Big(1 - \frac{\ell - \ell_0}{\ell_1 - \ell_0},\ 0.1,\ 1\Big)

\ell is how far the vehicle is behind; \ell_0 = 0.15 m and \ell_1 = 0.40 m (0.05 and 0.25 rad for attitude). Past 1 m or 0.8 rad, the profile is re-seeded at the vehicle.

The optimal control problem

Explained in Chapter 5 · mpc.cpp

\begin{aligned} \min_{U}\quad & \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k\, \big\| y_k \big\|^2_{Q} + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} r\,\big\| \delta_k \big\|^2 + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} r_\Delta \big\| \delta_k - \delta_{k-1} \big\|^2 \\[4pt] \text{s.t.}\quad & x_0 = \Phi_{\text{delay}}(\hat x), \quad x_{k+1} = F(x_k, u_k), \quad |u_k| \le 24\ \text{N} \end{aligned}

with \delta_k = u_k - u^{\text{ff}}_k, the deviation from the feedforward. \delta_{-1} is the deviation of the command last sent, and w_k = 1, except w_N = 5.

Decision state

Explained in Chapter 5

\tilde x = (p,\ q,\ v,\ \omega,\ f), \qquad \text{tangent space } \R^{3+3+3+3+8} = \R^{20}

Errors

Explained in Chapter 5

y_k = \begin{bmatrix} p_b - p_{\text{ref}} \\ \operatorname{Log}\!\big(q_{\text{ref}}^{-1} \otimes q\big) \\ v_b - R(q)^\top v_{\text{ref}} \\ \omega - R(q)^\top \omega_{\text{ref}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{aligned} p_b &= p + R(q)\, r_{bl} \\ v_b &= v + \omega \times r_{bl} \end{aligned}

Errors are measured at base_link, offset r_{bl} from the COM. In velocity mode, the velocity rows compare with the commanded body velocity and the pose rows drop out.

Q = \operatorname{diag}\big(\underbrace{400\,I_3}_{\text{position}},\ \underbrace{3000,\,3000,\,300}_{\text{roll, pitch, yaw}},\ \underbrace{20\,I_3}_{\text{velocity}},\ \underbrace{10\,I_3}_{\text{rates}}\big)

Feedforward

Explained in Chapter 5

\btau^{\text{ff}}_k = \M\big(\dot{\bnu}_{\text{ref}} - \dot{\bnu}\,\big|_{x_{\text{ref}},\ \tau = 0}\big), \qquad u^{\text{ff}}_k = \operatorname{clip}\big(\T^{+}\btau^{\text{ff}}_k\big)

sampled half a stage plus one rise time ahead, so the lagged thrust lands on time.

Delay compensation

Explained in Chapter 5

\Phi_{\text{delay}} rolls the estimate \hat x forward 30 ms through a copy of the thruster replica, which holds every command still in flight. The reference runs on the same clock.

The solver

Explained in Chapter 6 · mpc.cpp, box_qp.cpp

On the manifold

\begin{aligned} x \boxplus \delta &= \big(p + \delta_p,\ q \otimes \operatorname{Exp}(\delta_\theta),\ v + \delta_v,\ \omega + \delta_\omega,\ f + \delta_f\big) \\ a \boxminus b &= \big(p_a - p_b,\ \operatorname{Log}(q_b^{-1} \otimes q_a),\ v_a - v_b,\ \omega_a - \omega_b,\ f_a - f_b\big) \end{aligned}

Linearize

Explained in Chapter 6

Roll out \bar x_{k+1} = F(\bar x_k, \bar u_k) from the warm start. Then, column by column with \varepsilon = 10^{-6}:

F(\bar x_k \boxplus \delta x,\ \bar u_k + \delta u) \boxminus F(\bar x_k, \bar u_k) \approx A_k\, \delta x + B_k\, \delta u, \qquad A_k \in \R^{20\times 20},\ B_k \in \R^{20\times 8}

Condense

Explained in Chapter 6

P_0 = 0, \qquad P_{k+1} = A_k P_k + \big[\,0 \ \cdots\ B_k\ \cdots\ 0\,\big], \qquad M_k = \frac{\partial y_k}{\partial \delta x}\, P_k
\begin{aligned} H &= \textstyle\sum_{k} w_k M_k^\top Q M_k + r I + r_\Delta D^\top D \\ g &= \textstyle\sum_{k} w_k M_k^\top Q\, y_k + r\,\delta + r_\Delta D^\top (D\,\delta - e_0) \end{aligned}

D is the stage-difference operator; e_0 carries \delta_{-1} into the first stage.

Box QP

Explained in Chapter 6

\min_{\Delta U}\ \tfrac12\, \Delta U^\top H\, \Delta U + g^\top \Delta U \qquad \text{s.t.}\qquad -24 - U \le \Delta U \le 24 - U

Projected Newton, repeated until the free gradient is below 10^{-8} (50 iterations at most):

\begin{aligned} \nabla &= H\,\Delta U + g \\ \mathcal B &= \{\, i : \Delta U_i \text{ at a bound}, \ \nabla_i \text{ outward} \,\} \\ H_{ff}\, d_f &= -\nabla_f, \qquad d_{\mathcal B} = 0 \quad (\text{Cholesky}) \\ \Delta U &\leftarrow \Pi\big(\Delta U + \alpha d\big), \quad \alpha = 1, \tfrac12, \tfrac14, \dots \end{aligned}

with \alpha halved until the projected Armijo condition holds.

Real-time iteration

Explained in Chapter 6

U \leftarrow U + \Delta U, \qquad \text{send } u_0, \qquad U_{\text{warm}} = (u_1, \dots, u_{N-1}, u_{N-1})

One linearize-and-solve per 50 ms tick. The first tick, and any tick after a reset, starts from U^{\text{ff}}. The whole tick as pseudocode is in Chapter 9.

Disturbance

Explained in Chapter 7

\begin{aligned} \hat d_F &\leftarrow \hat d_F + \gamma\, \frac{R(q)\, \M_{v}\, e_v}{\tau_F}, & |\hat d_F| &\le 20\ \text{N} \\ \hat d_\tau &\leftarrow \hat d_\tau + \gamma\, \frac{\M_{\omega}\, e_\omega}{\tau_\tau}, & |\hat d_\tau| &\le 5\ \text{N·m} \end{aligned}
\gamma = \frac{1}{1 + (|v| / 0.1\ \text{m/s})^2} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + (|\omega| / 0.2\ \text{rad/s})^2}, \qquad \tau_F = \tau_\tau = 3\ \text{s}

e_v, e_\omega are the innovations, and \M_v, \M_\omega the translational and rotational blocks of \M. \hat d enters every prediction stage and the feedforward as \btau_d.

Settle trust

Explained in Chapter 7

\text{trust} \leftarrow \begin{cases} 1 - e^{-\Delta t/0.25}\,(1 - \text{trust}) & \text{settled} \\ \text{trust}\cdot e^{-\Delta t / 1.0} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}

Identification

Explained in Chapter 8 · pool_identify

Thrust impulse between samples, from the commanded forces through the thruster model:

j_k = \int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_k} \T\, f(t)\, dt

Statics, from holds at several attitudes

Explained in Chapter 8

B = \frac1K \sum_k \big(W - \hat u_k \cdot \bar f_k\big), \qquad \begin{bmatrix} [\hat u_1]_\times \\ \vdots \\ [\hat u_K]_\times \end{bmatrix} \mathrm p = \begin{bmatrix} \bar\tau_1 \\ \vdots \\ \bar\tau_K \end{bmatrix}, \qquad r_b = \mathrm p / B

\hat u_k is world "up" in the body at hold k; \bar f_k, \bar\tau_k are the mean thrust force and torque. An SVD of the stacked matrix reports which directions of r_b the holds actually observed.

Drag, from steady runs

Explained in Chapter 8

\tau - \tau_{\text{hold}}(\hat u) = D_1\, v + D_2\, |v|\, v, \qquad D_1, D_2 \ge 0

Added mass, from the acceleration windows

Explained in Chapter 8

M\,(v_j - v_i) = \big(C(t_j) - C(t_i)\big) - \sum_{k=i+1}^{j} \big(D_1 \bar v_k + D_2 |\bar v_k| \bar v_k\big)\, \Delta t_k

C(t) is the cumulative excess impulse \sum (j_k - \tau_{\text{hold}}\,\Delta t_k). One line through the origin over all windows gives the total M. The fit is repeated with the thrust delayed by 0 to 0.6 s; if a delay beyond 0.25 s fits better, the "mass" is really lag and is rejected. Accepted: R^2 \ge 0.8, added mass between −0.25 and 5 times the rigid value (3 for rotations).
